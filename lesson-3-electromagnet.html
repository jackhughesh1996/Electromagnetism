Lesson 3: Electromagnets (KS3 / Year 8 Physics)

An electromagnet is a type of magnet in which the magnetic field is produced by an electric current. Unlike permanent bar magnets, electromagnets can be switched on and off instantly!

The Three Ways to Strengthen an Electromagnet

You can make any electromagnet stronger by:

  1. Increasing the Electric Current (I) — Add more cells/batteries or turn up the power supply.
  2. Increasing the Number of Turns (N) — Wind more tight coils of insulated copper wire around the core.
  3. Using a Soft Iron Core — Soft iron is easily magnetised and concentrates the magnetic flux lines by hundreds of times!

Comparison: Permanent Magnets vs Electromagnets

Property Permanent Bar Magnet Electromagnet
Switchable? No, always magnetic Yes! Turn on/off with a switch
Variable Strength? Fixed strength Adjust current or coil turns
Reversible Poles? Fixed North and South Reverse current direction to swap poles
Needs Energy? No external energy needed Requires a continuous electrical current

Why Soft Iron instead of Steel?

Soft Iron is a magnetically soft material. It magnetises quickly when the current is turned on, but demagnetises immediately when the current is turned off.

If we used steel (a magnetically hard material), it would retain its magnetism permanently even after opening the switch, meaning a scrap crane could never drop the cars it picks up!

Real-World Applications

• Scrap-yard cranes: Powerful electromagnets pick up tons of scrap steel and drop them into crushers simply by switching off the current.
• Electric bells: The electromagnet repeatedly attracts an iron armature, ringing the gong and breaking the circuit in a cycle.
• Maglev trains: Electromagnets levitate trains above tracks to eliminate friction and achieve 300+ mph speeds.

📋 5-Step Guided Practical: Electric Motor Explorer

Discover how interacting magnetic fields create sustained rotation in a DC motor.

CHALLENGE 1 Two Magnetic Fields

1. Find the Two Magnetic Fields & The Single Wire Force

Before a motor can turn, two distinct magnetic fields must interact:
1. Permanent Magnet Field
Uniform translucent field lines running straight from the red North pole to the blue South pole across the motor gap.
2. Coil Electromagnet Field
Formed by current looping through the coil turns, turning the rectangular coil itself into an electromagnet with its own North and South poles!
Core Rule: Current in a wire produces a magnetic field. When this wire is placed inside an existing external magnetic field, the two magnetic fields push against each other — this is the Motor Effect!
CHALLENGE 2 Forces in 3D

2. Where Are the Forces? 3D Vectors & 2D Symbols

Look closely at the rectangular coil:
  • Electric current travels in opposite directions along the two long sides of the rectangular coil.
  • Because the magnetic field (N ➔ S) is the same for both sides, the resulting Lorentz force pushes the left side UP and the right side DOWN!
Understanding Conventional 2D Diagrams:
Many textbook diagrams use 2D symbols because paper cannot show depth:
• • = Point pointing directly TOWARDS you (like the tip of an approaching arrow).
• × = Cross pointing directly AWAY from you (like the fletching feathers of an arrow flying away).
CHALLENGE 3 Creating Rotation

3. Make the Motor Turn: Force Pair ➔ Turning Effect (Torque)

When one side of the coil is pushed up and the opposite side is pushed down at equal distances from the central axle, they form a couple (a pair of equal and opposite forces).

This pair creates a sustained turning effect (torque) that forces the axle to rotate. Using the Step 45° button or Slow Motion, you can inspect how the force vectors stay vertical while the coil rotates through 360°!

CHALLENGE 4 The Crucial Component

4. The Commutator Challenge: What Happens Without It?

Why does a DC motor need a split-ring commutator?
Try it in the 3D Simulator:
1. Uncheck Split-Ring Commutator in the left control panel.
2. Watch what happens: the coil rotates until it reaches the vertical alignment (90°), then slows down, reverses, and starts oscillating back and forth before coming to a complete stop!
3. Re-enable the split-ring commutator: the motor immediately resumes continuous full-circle rotation!

The Explanation: Every half-turn, the two sides of the coil swap positions. Without a commutator, the forces would now push backwards, pulling the coil back to vertical. The split-ring commutator switches the electrical connections every half-turn (180°), ensuring that whichever wire is on the left is always pushed UP, maintaining continuous rotation in one direction!

📖 Year 8 KS3 Science: Electric Motor Theory

Fleming’s Left-Hand Rule, the Motor Effect, and real-world DC motors.

Fleming’s Left-Hand Rule

Fleming's Left-Hand Rule allows you to predict the direction of force on a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field:

Hold your left hand with thumb, first finger, and second finger all at right angles (90°) to each other:

• Thumb: Direction of Motion / Force (F)
• First Finger: Direction of Magnetic Field (B) (North to South)
• Second Finger: Direction of Current (I) (Positive to Negative)

Memory Trick: Father (Thumb/Force), Mother (First/Magnetic Field), Child (Second/Current)!

The Motor Effect Formula: F = B × I × L

The magnitude of the force acting on the wire is directly proportional to three quantities:

$$ F = B \times I \times L $$
  • F: Force on the conductor (in Newtons, N)
  • B: Magnetic flux density of the permanent magnets (in Tesla, T)
  • I: Electric current flowing through the conductor (in Amperes, A)
  • L: Length of wire inside the magnetic field (in meters, m)
  • For a coil with N turns: Total Force = $N \times B \times I \times L$.

Role of the Split-Ring Commutator and Carbon Brushes

Component Description Key Function in DC Motor
Split-Ring Commutator A brass cylinder split into two insulated semicircular halves mounted on the spinning axle. Swaps the electrical connections to the coil every half-turn (180°) so current continually produces torque in the same direction.
Carbon Brushes Spring-loaded blocks of graphite that gently press against the spinning commutator ring. Provides smooth electrical contact from the stationary battery circuit to the rotating axle without tangling wires.
